Welcome to Outpost 7

Build a working base on the Moon or Mars. Keep your astronaut safe for 7 sols and do real science.

Real NASA data settings The game uses NASA's open APIs: solar flares (DONKI), near-Earth asteroids (NeoWs), Mars rover photos and Astronomy Picture of the Day. Paste your free key from api.nasa.gov. It is saved only in this browser, never in the game file.

How to play

There are some main components in the game

  • Make a working outpost (Lunar or Martian).
  • Every design is a trade-off. You only have so many cargo crates.
  • Do science to earn science points.
  • Size doesn't matter. The function and effectiveness of your outpost is more important.

The game

  • You control a Martian or Lunar astronaut. Click a tile (or use arrow keys) to walk.
  • Every sol has a countdown timer. Walking and work take time. You cannot skip the day: when the timer hits zero, night falls. Get back to the blue safe zone before then!
  • Tasks come in 5 levels, set by their points. Harder tasks take longer and use more power: Very easy (5 points, 2 s, 1 power), Easy (10 to 15 points, 3 s, 2 power), Medium (20 points, 8 s, 5 power), Hard (25 points, 11 s, 7 power) and Challenge (30 points, 15 s, 10 power).
  • Pick a material for each module: Aluminum (standard), 3D-printed regolith (made from local soil: fewer crates, slower, extra shielding), Polyethylene + water (strong radiation shield, but costs more crates and power) or Kevlar inflatable (light and fast, but weak). You can change a module's material later with Change a module's material. A small tag on each tile shows its material.
  • Stuck or curious? Click Ask Mission Control and type a space question. Not sure what to do next? Press 📞 Call Earth for hints about your outpost (3 calls each sol). The game pauses while you read.
  • Storms hit most nights. Mars: dust storms, dust devils, great dust storms, deep cold and radiation storms. Moon: solar flares, coronal mass ejections, micrometeorites, meteor showers and moonquakes. A weather station shows tomorrow's storm.
  • Real space weather: each sol replays one real day from the latest weekly NOAA snapshot, taken every Monday. The home screen shows what got better and worse since last week. You'll see that day's solar flares, and on the geomagnetic storm day the radio link to Earth is slower.
  • Some modules are required to win, and the list grows with the mode. Cadet needs a habitat and power. Junior adds a comms dish, Commander adds a greenhouse, and Veteran adds a med bay. Every module uses crates and power (3 power per crate). Open the build menu whenever you are ready. Walking around never makes you build.
  • Choose where to place things. Walls and water tanks only shield you if they touch the rocket or a habitat.
  • Keep 3 meters up: Radiation shield, Life support and Power. Life support is made of three parts, 💧 water, 🫁 oxygen and 🍎 food, and the meter shows their average. Water and oxygen drop 16 a night, food drops 12. These match NASA's daily numbers for one astronaut: about 4 L of water (3 L to drink, 1 L to wash), 0.84 kg of oxygen and 1.8 kg of food. Growing plants and lab work use some water too. If one of them runs out, the crew is in crisis and the other two drain fast every night. The mission ends when the life support meter or the radiation shield hits zero. Power is your fuel: walking uses 1 power every 2 steps, and hot water for washing uses 4 every night. The power tank holds 150. If power runs out at night, water freezes and the air recycler stops, so water and oxygen drop fast.
  • Walking outside the blue safe zone lowers your radiation shield. All three meters are better when they are higher: a green ▲ is good news, a red ▼ is bad news. Storms and solar flares hit at night.
  • Survive 7 sols and reach the science target to win. There are 5 game modes, from Cadet (easiest) to Veteran (hardest), plus Free play where you cannot lose.
